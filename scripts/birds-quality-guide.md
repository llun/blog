# Bird photo KEEP / HIDE guide

Learned from the owner's corrections to 420 model-picked bursts in 10 albums: 367 left as picked (87%), 41 whole bursts hidden, 8 with extra frames kept, 4 keeper switched. Default: one keeper per burst, and the usual pick (sharp, bird visible) is right. Only the rules below change that. Counts are the evidence; do not add rules that have none.

## HIDE the whole burst (41 cases)
The owner judges a burst against its neighbours: 35 of the 41 hidden bursts had another burst of the same bird on the same perch that stayed. Hide when one of these holds:

1. **Head or face cannot be seen, and a neighbouring burst shows the bird better (25).** Head inside the nest hole or only the tail sticking out (5), rear view from below or preening with the head tucked (7), flying away from the camera (3), head buried in grass and soft (3), bent over in shade (2), most of the bird behind a trunk (4), bird buried in twigs (1).
2. **Repeat of a kept neighbour: same bird, same perch, same pose, nothing new (12).** The frame itself can be fine. Example: four bursts of the same three swallows on one tree, two stayed.
3. **Tiny dark speck with no colour or markings (4).** Black flight silhouettes on grey sky (3) and a dot in a field (1), bird about 1-3% of the frame width. These were hidden with no better neighbour.

How many of a run stay: when every burst of the scene is weak, one or two (blue tit seen from behind, 1 of 6; woodpecker with head in the hole, 2 of 6). When the frames are good and the poses differ, most (godwit feeding, 5 of 6).

## Not a reason to hide
- **A weak shot that is the only record of the scene stays.** Kept as picked: a woodpecker with its head in the hole, a blue tit from behind, a distant buzzard on a fence (6 of 7 bursts), two tiny swallows on a wire.
- **Motion blur on an action frame.** Kept 3 times: a swallow in flight, a woodpecker leaving the hole, a chaffinch taking off.
- **Small bird whose colour and markings still show.** Godwits in flight at about 5% of the frame width stayed (5 bursts).
- No correction in this data was about a bird cut off by the frame edge or a plain out-of-focus frame. Do not infer a rule for those.

## KEEP more than one frame of a burst (8 cases)
Only when the bird is in clear view and lit (8 of 8) and the frames show different things:

- a different head pose, or bill open (calling) against bill closed (5);
- a different action: taking off and perched, pecking a bud and looking up, two wing positions in flight (3).

Two frames in 6 cases, three in 1, four in 1. Never more than four, even from bursts of 20-25 frames. The model's own pick was always among the frames kept, so add to it, do not replace it. Frames that differ only by a small shift are still one keeper.

## Which frame to prefer (4 switches)
1. The frame where the eye is visible and the face is turned to the camera (3 of 4): eye and bill showing over only the crown; face-on over looking away; a clean profile with a clear eye over an open bill with the eye lost.
2. A lit bird with colour over a dark silhouette, even with the head hidden in both (1 of 4).

Sharpness was about equal in all four, so it did not decide. Eye and face beat an open bill or an "action" look.

## Applying it
- **Single photo:** HIDE only a featureless dark speck, or a bird so covered that head and body cannot be made out. Otherwise KEEP.
- **Burst:** keep the frame with the clearest eye and face. Add a second to fourth frame only for a clearly different head pose or action.
- **Several bursts of one scene:** keep the best. Hide the ones where the head is not visible and the ones that repeat a kept pose. Leave one, even a weak one, so the scene is not lost.
